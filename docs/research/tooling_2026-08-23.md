# Research digest — tooling — 2026-08-23

## Bottom line
Two genuine candidates this pass: one fills a real HTTP/3-specific race-condition gap that maps directly onto our #1 top-tech (HTTP/3 is our most common in-scope fingerprint and we have zero HTTP/3-aware tooling), the other is a mature Rust hidden-param tool that beats Arjun on one specific axis (semantic default-value guessing) without replacing it. One SKIP for maturity reasons.

### 1. EVALUATE — `cyberark/QuicDrawH3` (CyberArk Labs, now Palo Alto Networks) — HTTP/3 race-condition fuzzer
- **What it is:** Python tool (`pip install quicdraw`, optional `[ui]` GUI) with its own minimal TLS 1.3 + QUIC + HTTP/3 stack. Implements `Quic-Fin-Sync` — a single-packet-style race-condition primitive built for HTTP/3 (over QUIC) instead of HTTP/2's Single-Packet-Attack. Also does raw FUZZ+wordlist fuzzing over HTTP/3 with custom headers and SSLKEYLOGFILE support for Wireshark inspection.
- **Why it matters to us specifically:** our own in-scope top-tech list leads with **HTTP/3** — and every race-condition tool we've evaluated (Raceocat, RacePWN, Turbo Intruder-style single-packet attacks) targets HTTP/1.1 pipelining or HTTP/2 multiplexing, not QUIC/H3. If a meaningful slice of our HTTP/3-fronted hosts (Cloudflare/CDN-heavy estate) terminate H3 at the app rather than just the edge, our existing race-condition posture (KB: `class-race-conditions.md`) is blind to them structurally, not just under-tooled.
- **Doctrine fit:** detection-shaped, not destructive by design (it races/fuzzes to observe timing windows, doesn't need to complete an exploit to prove the primitive exists) — same posture as our other confirm-primitives. Vendor-published research (CyberArk Labs), Apache-2.0.
- **Caveats:** young/low-traction (23 stars, no visible commit-cadence signal beyond the repo existing) — this is closer to a research release than a battle-tested scanner. Also only useful where the origin app (not just the CDN edge) actually terminates H3 — needs a quick check against our real HTTP/3 hosts before wiring in.
- **Verdict: EVALUATE** — trial against 2-3 of our confirmed HTTP/3 hosts (read-only race probes only, same rate-limit discipline as `recon_smuggling.sh`-style lanes) before deciding on a permanent lane. Do not autonomous-run yet given its youth.

### 2. EVALUATE (complement, not replace, Arjun) — `Sh1Yo/x8` — Rust hidden-parameter discovery
- **What it is:** Rust hidden-param suite, 2.1k★, actively developed (issue traffic Aug 2026, releases through v3.1.0). Line-by-line diffing + response-code + reflection comparison, and — the differentiator — it can surface parameters by their **effect on the response even with non-random/semantic values** (its README example is discovering `admin=true`-style params), which Arjun's wordlist+reflection model is weaker at.
- **Why it matters:** our "active param discovery" lane doctrine is explicitly about finding params that "drive SSRF/cache-poison/reflected bugs the crowd's archive-only tooling misses" — a param whose *value*, not just its *name*, flips app behavior (privesc/BAC-flavored, e.g. `admin=true`, `debug=1`) is exactly the class Arjun structurally can't find (it detects param *presence*, not param *semantics*). Also much faster (Rust, thousands of URLs/run) — useful for the on-demand `crawl-host` path where wall-clock matters.
- **Doctrine fit:** unauth-safe if run GET-only/rate-limited like Arjun already is; dup-resistant in the sense that almost nobody runs value-semantic param discovery, only name discovery.
- **Verdict: EVALUATE as a second pass alongside Arjun** in `recon-params arjun <host>` (same on-demand-only, non-autonomous gate) — not a replacement, since the two tools' hit sets don't fully overlap.

### SKIP — `JavanXD/Raceocat`
Browser-extension-driven (Firefox extension → local dispatcher), 26★/9 forks, last real activity tied to a 2022 conference paper — not built for unattended/CLI pipeline use and has no traction advantage over what we already track for race-condition classes. Not worth wiring in.

### Checked, no action
- **jwt_tool / jwtXploiter / JWT Security Analyzer** — all mature-but-nothing-new; `jwt_tool` remains the de facto standard and we already reference `class-jwt-attacks.md` conceptually — no gap here worth a dedicated lane change.
- **Scalpel Racer** — still the single-author/unverified-traction tool flagged SKIP on 2026-07-28; no new maturity signal this pass, staying SKIP.
- **RacePWN, h2spacex** — legacy/low-signal race tooling, superseded in relevance by QuicDrawH3 for our actual (HTTP/3-heavy) estate.
